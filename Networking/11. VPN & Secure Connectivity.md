<h1>Chapter 11 — VPN &amp; Secure Connectivity</h1>

<p>
A <strong>VPN (Virtual Private Network)</strong> provides a secure way
to communicate over an untrusted network such as the Internet.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>11.1 VPN Fundamentals</h2>

<p>
<strong>VPN = Virtual Private Network</strong>
</p>

<p>
A VPN creates a <strong>secure and encrypted connection</strong> over a
network that may not be trusted, such as the Internet.
</p>

<p>
For example, an employee working from home can securely access company
resources through a VPN.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Simple meaning:</strong>
VPN = A secure connection between you and another network or device
over an untrusted network.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>11.2 VPN Tunnel</h2>

<p>
A <strong>VPN tunnel</strong> is the protected communication path created
between VPN endpoints.
</p>

<p>
Network traffic is protected and carried through this tunnel.
</p>

<pre>
Employee
   ↓
VPN Tunnel
   ↓
Company Network
</pre>

<p>
The Internet may be between the endpoints, but the VPN provides a
protected path for the communication.
</p>

<p>
<strong>VPN Tunnel = Protected path through an untrusted network.</strong>
</p>

<hr>

<h2>11.3 Remote Access VPN</h2>

<p>
A <strong>Remote Access VPN</strong> allows an individual user to securely
connect to an organization's network from a remote location.
</p>

<pre>
Employee Laptop
       ↓
    Internet
       ↓
  VPN Gateway
       ↓
Company Network
</pre>

<p>Commonly used by:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Remote Employees</li>
  <li>IT Administrators</li>
  <li>Support Staff</li>
</ul>

<p>
The user normally authenticates before getting access to the network
or permitted resources.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>11.4 Site-to-Site VPN</h2>

<p>
A <strong>Site-to-Site VPN</strong> connects two entire networks rather
than connecting only one user.
</p>

<pre>
Office A
   │
VPN Tunnel
   │
Office B
</pre>

<p>Common examples:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Head Office → Branch Office</li>
  <li>Company Network → Data Center</li>
</ul>

<p>
Users in both networks can communicate through the VPN connection.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Remote Access VPN → User to Network</strong><br>
<strong>Site-to-Site VPN → Network to Network</strong>
</p>

<hr>

<h2>11.5 IPsec Basics</h2>

<p>
<strong>IPsec = Internet Protocol Security</strong>
</p>

<p>
IPsec is a collection of protocols and mechanisms used to secure
IP communication.
</p>

<p>IPsec can provide:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Encryption</li>
  <li>Authentication</li>
  <li>Integrity</li>
</ul>

<p>
IPsec is commonly used with <strong>Site-to-Site VPNs</strong> and can
also be used for remote-access VPNs.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Simple meaning:</strong>
IPsec = Technology used to protect IP traffic.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>11.6 SSL VPN</h2>

<p>
An <strong>SSL VPN</strong> uses TLS/SSL-based security to provide
secure remote access.
</p>

<p>
It is commonly used by remote users to access company resources.
</p>

<pre>
User
 ↓
Internet
 ↓
SSL VPN
 ↓
Company Resources
</pre>

<p>
Depending on the implementation, an SSL VPN may be accessed through
a web browser or a VPN client.
</p>

<h3>IPsec VPN vs SSL VPN</h3>

<p>
<strong>IPsec VPN → Commonly used for network-level VPN connections</strong>
</p>

<p>
<strong>SSL VPN → Commonly used for secure remote access</strong>
</p>

<hr>

<h2>11.7 VPN Gateway</h2>

<p>
A <strong>VPN Gateway</strong> is a device or service that establishes
and manages VPN connections.
</p>

<p>It can handle tasks such as:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Authentication</li>
  <li>Encryption</li>
  <li>VPN tunnel establishment</li>
  <li>VPN traffic handling</li>
</ul>

<h3>Remote Access Example</h3>

<pre>
Remote User
     ↓
Internet
     ↓
VPN Gateway
     ↓
Internal Network
</pre>

<h3>Site-to-Site Example</h3>

<pre>
Office A
  ↓
VPN Gateway
  ↓
VPN Tunnel
  ↓
VPN Gateway
  ↓
Office B
</pre>

<p>
In a Site-to-Site VPN, there can be a VPN gateway on each side.
</p>
<hr>
<h2>🎯 SOC L1 Connection</h2>
<p>
VPN logs can be useful during security investigations because they can
provide information about remote access to an organization's network.
</p>

<p>Depending on the VPN solution, logs may contain:</p>

<ul>
  <li>User identity</li>
  <li>Connection time</li>
  <li>Source IP</li>
  <li>VPN-assigned IP</li>
  <li>Connection status</li>
  <li>Session duration</li>
</ul>

<h3>Example VPN Log</h3>

<pre>
User:        employee01
Source IP:   Public IP
VPN IP:      10.10.10.25
Status:      Successful
Time:        09:15
</pre>

<p>
This information can help an analyst determine
<strong>who connected to the organization's network and when</strong>.
</p>

<p>
VPN logs can therefore be useful for
<strong>user attribution, IP correlation, and incident investigation.</strong>
</p>
